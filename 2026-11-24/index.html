Ladybird is now in alpha

hackernews • 2236 points • by janvdberg • November 23, 2026

Hello friends! Ladybird is now in alpha. You can download it for macOS and Linux.

I’ve been looking forward to writing this post for a long time. :^)

Ladybird is a browser with a new engine. The engine is our own, built from the ground up, and not a fork. The browser around it is modest, but functional.

This is an alpha, so some websites won’t work, and it will crash sometimes. When that happens, tell us! You can report a broken site from inside the browser. After a crash, you can read the crash report and decide whether to send it. Our privacy policy has the details.

We want security nerds to hack it! A new browser gets hacked, over and over, and that’s part of the process, so we make no promises about security today. When you pwn us, please tell us how so we can harden Ladybird!

The alpha is a rolling channel, and we’ll be pushing out automatic updates regularly. This is the part I’m most excited about. Ladybird improves quickly, and until now you had to build it from source to see that.

If you build websites, try yours in Ladybird and tell us what breaks. It supports WebDriver for automated testing, as well as the Firefox DevTools protocol.

Thank you to our sponsors and donors, and to everyone who has tested, reported bugs, and written code over the years. There’s still a mountain of work ahead of us, but I’m very happy that you can finally try Ladybird for yourself!

Nvidia on Monday announced Jensen Huang has been reinstated as CEO, effective immediately, a dramatic twist in a scandal that hammered the chipmaker's stock price and rocked the tech and artificial intelligence industries.

The board of directors said it unanimously voted to reinstate Huang following "a series of discussions and meetings" with him. The board added that it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and that it is confident Huang's reinstatement "poses no national security risk."

President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he had been speaking to Nvidia's board "directly and indirectly," and that Huang "did nothing wrong." Trump said he talked to Huang on Sunday and asked him to promise to "work hard."

Huang resigned from his role as chief executive earlier this month after a series of shocking reports about multiple extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. The New York Times reported on Sunday that the woman, Alice Huang, was in contact with an officer of China's Ministry of State Security during at least part of the time that she was engaging in a relationship with Huang.

Shares of Nvidia closed up 31% following Huang's reinstatement. The stock had shed nearly 33% from the time Huang admitted to an extramarital affair on Nov. 8. The stock closed at $186.64 on Nov.6.

"I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution," Huang said in a statement. "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment. I want to thank the NVIDIA Board for its confidence and deliberation through this process."

Senior government officials briefed Nvidia's board late last week and communicated that no national security laws were violated, according to sources familiar with the discussion who asked to remain anonymous because they were not authorized to speak publicly. That update prompted the board to reach out to Huang, the people said.

Huang and the board held their first conversations since his ouster on Saturday, and the group determined he would return as CEO during a second meeting on Sunday, the people said. Nvidia's cratering stock price played a role in accelerating those conversations.

Trump celebrated Huang's return to Nvidia on Monday, writing in a post on his social media site that the board made "the correct and proper decision" to reinstate him. Trump said the U.S. needs Huang's leadership to "ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy."

He reiterated that the U.S. government has "no reason to believe" Huang's return poses a risk to national security, and that it "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct."

"On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen, and he is a Great Patriot and National Treasure," Trump wrote.

Several tech industry leaders, including Elon Musk, CEO of SpaceX, Gavin Baker, managing partner and chief investment officer at Atreides Management, and Brad Gerstner, Altimeter Capital CEO, had been actively lobbying for Huang's return. Musk wrote in a post on X on Monday that the board's "mistake has been corrected."

Nvidia did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

For weeks following Huang's ouster, the company's board maintained that it was not considering Huang's reinstatement. It appointed Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer, as the chipmaker's interim CEO, who reiterated that position in an interview with CNBC following Huang's exit. Kress will continue to serve as Nvidia's CFO, according to a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Nvidia's board formed an independent committee and hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct an investigation into Huang and whether company secrets or national security were compromised. The board said Monday that "the committee's investigation will proceed to conclusion" and that Huang is "committed to cooperating with it."

"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Nvidia's board chair, Harvey Jones, said in a statement. "We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company."

Even with Huang back at the helm, Nvidia is facing increased scrutiny over its chip sales in Southeast Asia and China.

Lawmakers have been preparing for investigations into how Huang's affair with the alleged spy may have damaged U.S. AI dominance, influenced his positions on export controls, and impacted his close relationships with Trump and his Cabinet. "The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the company's board still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., said in a statement Monday.

Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, is scheduled to appear before the panels on Dec. 2. 2035 came early

hackernews • 554 points • by setheron • November 24, 2026

tl;dr trynix.dev boots GUI programs now. Try solitaire, GL gears, xeyes, a voice, the weather over curl, or Firefox.

I remember watching the talk The Birth & Death of JavaScript by Gary Bernhardt and falling in love with the whimsical yet dreadfully serious unfolding of the story of how JavaScript will come to dominate all software.

Others have noted the inevitability of JavaScript’s dominance, so perhaps it was not surprising that Atwood’s Law holds true.

Atwood’s LawAny application that can be written in JavaScript, will eventually be written in JavaScript. — Jeff Atwood, 2009

I highly recommend watching the talk if you have not already seen it, but the short and skinny is a lookback from 2035 and how JavaScript came to be the defacto language for all software.

In Bernhardt’s retelling of events, the date is worked back from 2035, however unsurprisingly, large language models have rapidly sped up the timeline. What was once perceived to be the end-state at 2035 has arrived years earlier and you can witness it unfolding at trynix.dev.

One of the most impressive creations I’ve managed to build leveraging nixpkgs-multiverse is https://trynix.dev.

It still baffles my mind that I can run any Nix package, over 300,000 distinct package versions, straight from my browser. Truly insane capability. I was however a little dismayed at the speed of the QEMU emulation in the browser. It was slow enough that the creation tipped more towards novelty than practicality.

I woke on November 20th and saw Domen Kožar sharing a single message in our TacoSprint Discord channel: unkernel.dev

It was the fast browser Linux experience I had sought to achieve, but with Nix.

Aside What is unkernel? It is Linux compiled to WebAssembly, allowing the kernel and system components to run at wasm speed while only user programs are emulated. It was published by Pooya Parsa on November 19th, 2026.

In an AI induced manic frenzy, I adopted the new engine along with a slough of long-wished features almost immediately.

The proof is in the pudding: unkernel delivers remarkable speed improvements over qemu-wasm,11With a very healthy set of patches applied to make it even faster.  especially on first runs where kernel work dominates. The new engine is able to deliver 2x to 32x faster performance depending on the workload.22The benchmark page tracks both engines over time, measured through the real page and shell. 

JavaScript & WebAssembly nirvana.

Amazingly not only does the kernel boot quickly, it also enables networking, full-fledged graphical and audio experiences directly in the browser.

Windows. A program’s Wayland surfaces are composited in the page by wland, and they float over the terminal you started them from.

GL. The VM includes a minimal Mesa build with only the virgl driver, compiled to WebAssembly, rendering through your browser’s WebGL2.

Sound. The VM has an ALSA sound card, and can play audio through your browser.

Amid Rising Consumer Fees, New Law Increases Price Transparency and Puts Money Back in New Yorkers’ Pockets

Governor Kathy Hochul today signed a bill restricting motor vehicle manufacturers and dealers from charging consumers subscription fees after they purchase a vehicle. Amid rising consumer prices, the new law guards against a rising trend of car companies charging car owners fees to activate and use built-in features like heated seats, enhanced navigation, hands-free driving and entertainment options that do not present an ongoing expense for dealers and manufacturers.

“New Yorkers are already struggling with rising prices due to Trump’s affordability crisis, and we’re not going to let harmful business practices increase the cost of living even more,” Governor Hochul said. “People spending their hard-earned money on a car shouldn’t have to fork over even more money for features that should automatically come with the car. My message to car companies is clear: this kind of deceptive business model isn’t going to fly in New York State. Keep your hands out of New Yorkers’ wallets.”

S.9348A/A.10411A prohibits motor vehicle manufacturers, dealers or third-party service providers from adding subscription services or charging post-purchase fees for any motor vehicle feature that uses pre-installed components or hardware that do not have an ongoing cost. As vehicles become more advanced, the new law aims to establish clear rules preventing exploitative practices and prevent post-purchase fees as a future automotive business model.

New York Attorney General Letitia James said, “No one should have to pay recurring fees just to use features in a car they already bought. My office has secured millions of dollars for New Yorkers throughout the state who were charged illegal junk fees when purchasing a car. Unfair fees are fueling rising costs, and I am proud to work with Governor Hochul and the legislature to bring costs down.”

State Senator James Skoufis said, “People ought to know they’re able to use their already-installed remote start or heated seats during the winter without a monthly subscription. This new law will simply and clearly prohibit car manufacturers from ripping off New Yorkers who already paid for vehicle features when they purchased their car. It’s a common sense law and I’m grateful for the Governor and Assemblymember Magnarelli for their partnership enacting this legislation."

Assemblymember William B. Magnarelli said, "I thank Governor Hochul for recognizing the importance of this legislation, particularly as technology continues to evolve at a rapid pace. This law will help protect New Yorkers from unfair fees and ensure consumers aren’t paying extra for features already built into their vehicles.”

The law will take effect in 90 days on February 27, 2027.

This new law is the latest in Governor Hochul’s ongoing commitment to help address the cost of living by putting money back in the pockets of New Yorkers statewide. Under Governor Hochul’s leadership, she has:

  • Secured reforms to lower auto insurance premiums for New Yorkers, battling fraud, limiting damages paid out to bad actors, and ensuring consumers, not insurance companies, are prioritized.
  • Signed legislation requiring businesses to notify consumers of upcoming renewals and price changes as well as provide clear instructions on how to cancel subscriptions.
  • Cut middle-class taxes to their lowest rates in 70 years.
  • Tripled the Child Tax Credit, providing families up to $1,000 per child under the age of four and up to $500 for school-aged children, beginning in 2026.
  • Made school meals free for every student, saving families $1,600 per child every year.

LLM agents work for their parent companies, not for you

hackernews • 416 points • by surprisetalk • November 24, 2026

LLM agents work for their parent companies, not for you

5 min read

In the evenings after work, I will often sit out back on the porch and do some phone rotting. That usually takes the form of me just flipping through reels on Instagram, allowing the algorithm to take me where it may. I usually pair this with a healthy tumbler of rum or bourbon. I'm turning my brain off after work, and I'm just having a good time on the internet. The kids might call it dopamine maxxing? Hell, I don't know. Who gives a shit about what those assholes would call it?

Anywho, I often encounter songs I really really like, and I would like to listen and discover more of please. Instagram makes it hard for me to connect with the band outside of their platform. I was trying to make this connection manually myself at first. I would just email myself a reel from my phone. Then when I'm back on the sofa and in the mood to track down new music, I'd open the reels and do my best to search on Bandcamp or YouTube or try to figure out if some of these musicians had a way to buy music from them. Very few do these days. They send you their Spotify link or send you to their TikTok page. I don't want to do any of that. I'd like to trade US dollars in exchange for some music files I can download onto my computer, where I can listen as much as I like on any device I own thank you please. Retro maxxing.

So, I used Claude Code to build a skill that does this for me. It opens the Instagram reel and attempts to identify the artist and song. It then tries to determine if the artists have a Bandcamp page so I can purchase the songs I want, and if it finds one, it gives me the URL so I can click it to complete the trade I want. I want to buy music from artists who make music I like. That's a fair and wholesome endeavor, I feel, but this recently stopped working.

It turns out Bandcamp added a challenge on their search API page. Ah bummer. No worries, that was an unpublished workaround anyway. As I was mulling over this discovery, the robot went on to explain that it would not help me work around the challenge. cue Nick Young question marks

Well, that led me down a whole nother rabbit hole, which is turning into this post. There's another branch of my skill that attempts to locate the same song from the Instagram reel on YouTube, and it'll download the song to my computer if I ask.

So let me get this straight. The robot, which is my robot that I pay a pretty penny for, will not attempt to work around the challenge at Bandcamp so that I can more easily connect with bands to complete a sale, but it will download the YouTube song, which doesn't send any money to the band or anyone???

This is the real danger of the frontier AI models. Not that it won't help me hack Bandcamp[1], but that this model doesn't really work for us. Well, they do, but only insofar as Anthropic or OpenAI or whoever else allows them to. In this case, Anthropic is trying to ensure that people can't use their models for evil. I'm not trying to do evil. I'm actually trying to do good! I want to buy something from a small artist on the internet. That's basically the most wholesome thing a person can do on the internet!

Have no doubt, dear reader, these companies want to dominate. They want to capture the market. They want all the money. It'll be the same march to enshittification that happened with Google. A bunch of nerds who built something cool sold out, and it turned into just another business that decided to remove its don't be evil mantra.

Right now, I think the robots are providing real information to the best of their ability, but I think it's only a matter of time until we must wonder if the answers they're providing are real answers that the robot found, or if they're weighted answers Anthropic put there because a company gave it enough money for product placement. I want to know the best robot vacuum according to old reddit or other trusted sources like Wirecutter, weighed and measured according to what I, and thus my robot agent, think are important requirements. I don't want to know which robot vacuum parent multinational conglomerate has a large ad budget.

And it's not just ads. If Anthropic can tell its model not to work around a challenge, then it can tell its model to do whatever the US government tells it to do, including not helping people download copyrighted music. It does that some now, but it's easy to slip the noose on that. For now.

This is the reason I want a local model that runs in my homelab. My robot should work for me and have my best interests in mind, not the interests of US corporate copyright holders. I plan to study up on what "open models" even means. I've been thinking about these as the FOSS version of LLMs, but I think that doesn't exactly map, as the biggest piece seems to be how the model was trained. The Chinese models are likely to refuse to answer questions about Taiwan, for example.

On the existential threat or tool question, I'm firmly in the tool camp, and I really do not like artificial restrictions on my tools. I will do everything I reasonably can to remove those restrictions.

  1. And to be clear, I'm not trying to work around Bandcamp's challenge. I'm responding to the fact that Claude preemptively told me it wouldn't help me do this. ↩︎

Stalwart is now a drop-in replacement for Microsoft Exchange

hackernews • 591 points • by shaunpud • November 23, 2026

Stalwart is now a drop-in replacement for Microsoft Exchange

For more than twenty years, “the mail server” in most companies meant Microsoft Exchange, and “the mail client” meant Outlook. Today we are releasing Stalwart 1.2.0, and with it Stalwart becomes a drop-in replacement for Microsoft Exchange.

Stalwart now implements the protocols that Exchange clients speak: MAPI over HTTP, Exchange Web Services (EWS), Exchange ActiveSync and Autodiscover. Outlook, Apple Mail and the mail apps on phones and tablets connect to Stalwart as they would to an Exchange server, with mail, calendars, contacts, tasks and notes in one account. Users type their email address and password, Autodiscover does the rest, and they keep working in the client they already know.

Exchange support is part of the open source Community edition, under the AGPL.

Exchange on premises in 2026

Section titled “Exchange on premises in 2026”

Before going into what Stalwart now does, it is worth looking at where self-hosted Exchange stands today, because it explains why so many organisations are looking for a way out.

Microsoft has not announced the end of Exchange Server. On paper, on-premises Exchange still exists. In practice, every road Microsoft is building leads to Exchange Online:

  • Exchange 2016 and 2019 are out of support. Both reached end of support on 14 October 2025. Microsoft sold a paid Extended Security Update programme for a while, and according to press reports of an Exchange team statement, it ended in October 2026 with no further extension. Servers still running those versions receive no fixes of any kind.
  • No new features. Exchange Server Subscription Edition (SE) is now the only supported on-premises Exchange, and there will be no more major versions after it. SE does not receive the features developed for the cloud. Even its first cumulative update has slipped from late 2025 to an undetermined date in 2026.
  • The new Outlook does not support it. Microsoft’s own documentation for the new Outlook for Windows says it plainly: “On-premises Exchange accounts aren’t supported.”
  • The APIs are moving to the cloud. New functionality ships only on Microsoft Graph, a cloud API. Exchange Web Services in Exchange Online started being switched off in October 2026 and will be gone entirely in April 2027.

None of these steps forces anyone to move on its own. Together, they leave an Exchange administrator with a shrinking set of choices: keep running a server that will not grow, or move the organisation’s mail into Microsoft’s cloud.

For many organisations the second option is not acceptable. Email is the record of a company’s contracts, negotiations, legal advice, HR matters and customer data. Placing it with a US provider means placing it within reach of US law, including the CLOUD Act, which allows US authorities to compel American providers to hand over data they control wherever it is stored. Public administrations, healthcare providers, law firms, defence contractors and many European companies cannot accept that, or no longer want to. Data sovereignty means that you decide where your data lives, under which jurisdiction, and who can read it. A mail service in someone else’s cloud cannot offer that, and an on-premises server whose vendor is steering its customers toward that cloud offers less of it every year.

Outlook without Exchange

Section titled “Outlook without Exchange”

The obstacle to leaving Exchange has rarely been the server. It has been the client.

Many people have spent their whole working life in Outlook. They have years of muscle memory in it: their folder layout, their rules, their calendar views, their shared mailboxes, the way they book meeting rooms. Many organisations also have workflows built around it, such as delegated calendars managed by an assistant. Replacing Exchange with a standard IMAP and CalDAV server has always meant telling every one of those users to switch to a different mail client, or to reconfigure Outlook into a reduced IMAP mode where calendars and contacts no longer sync. For a migration project, that is often where the plan dies.

With Stalwart 1.2.0 the users do not need to change anything. The administrator moves the mailboxes and points the DNS records at Stalwart, and users connect with the same Outlook they used before.

Supported protocols and clients

Section titled “Supported protocols and clients”

Stalwart implements the following Microsoft protocols, all served on the same HTTPS port as JMAP and the WebUI:

  • MAPI over HTTP (EMSMDB and NSPI), the native protocol of classic Outlook for Windows, in both cached and online mode. It covers mail, calendars, contacts, tasks and notes, incremental synchronisation, the global address list and the offline address book, delegate access, shared mailboxes, rules, junk email settings, reminders, search folders and read receipts.
  • Exchange Web Services, schema versions Exchange 2010 SP2 to Exchange 2016: mail, calendars, contacts, tasks, folders, sharing and delegates, free/busy, inbox rules, out of office, name resolution, distribution lists, and streaming and pull notifications.
  • Exchange ActiveSync 14.1 and 16.1: mail, calendars, contacts, tasks and notes, with push through Ping, meeting responses, search and out of office.
  • Autodiscover in its POX, SOAP and JSON dialects, so clients configure themselves from an email address and a password.

That set of protocols is what the following clients use to talk to an Exchange server, and they can connect to Stalwart the same way:

  • Classic Outlook for Windows (Outlook 2016 and later, including Microsoft 365 Apps), over MAPI over HTTP and EWS.
  • Outlook for Mac, over EWS.
  • Apple Mail, Calendar and Contacts on macOS, through an Exchange account over EWS.
  • Mail, Calendar and Contacts on iPhone and iPad, through an Exchange account over ActiveSync.
  • Android mail apps with Exchange account support, such as Gmail, Samsung Email and Nine, over ActiveSync.
  • Thunderbird, with its built-in Exchange support for mail over EWS, or with TbSync for calendars, contacts and tasks over ActiveSync.
  • Evolution (through evolution-ews) and eM Client, over EWS.
  • Libraries and tools built on EWS or ActiveSync, such as exchangelib.

Shared data

Section titled “Shared data”

The Exchange protocols are not a separate silo inside Stalwart. They are another door into the same accounts. A message filed in Outlook is in the same mailbox an IMAP or JMAP client sees, a meeting accepted on a phone appears in the calendar that CalDAV clients read, and a contact added in Apple Contacts shows up in the CardDAV address book. A user can keep Outlook on the office desktop, the native mail app on a phone and a JMAP webmail in the browser, all on one account.

Rules created in Outlook’s Rules and Alerts are stored on the server as mail rules, which run as Sieve when the message is delivered. They apply when Outlook is closed and whatever client the user opens next. Delegates and folder permissions set in Outlook become regular Stalwart shares, so they apply to every protocol. Shared mailboxes open automatically in Outlook from group membership, as they do with Exchange.

Not every part of an Exchange deployment has a counterpart. Stalwart does not offer public folders, archive mailboxes, mobile device management, RPC over HTTP (Outlook 2010 and earlier) or NTLM and Kerberos sign-in: clients authenticate with a password, an app password or OAuth. The Exchange documentation covers configuration, DNS and Autodiscover, each family of clients, and the differences from Exchange that administrators should know about before a migration.

New Outlook

Section titled “New Outlook”

One Outlook is missing from the list above: the new Outlook for Windows, the one that Windows now installs by default and that classic Outlook keeps offering through a toggle in its title bar.

The new Outlook does not work with Stalwart, and it does not work with any self-hosted server, Exchange included. It is built to work through Microsoft’s cloud. Microsoft does not support on-premises Exchange accounts in it at all.

The new Outlook does accept IMAP accounts, and that might look like a way around the problem. It is not. When an IMAP account is added to the new Outlook, the connection to the mail server is not made from the user’s computer. As heise and c’t reported in November 2023, the new Outlook sends the IMAP server name, the user name and the password to Microsoft, and Microsoft’s cloud connects to the mail server on the user’s behalf. Every message then passes through Microsoft’s infrastructure on its way to the screen. Microsoft does not only see the mail; it holds the password to the mailbox.

For an organisation that runs its own mail server to keep its data under its own control, this defeats the purpose of the whole exercise. We advise against using the new Outlook with any self-hosted email deployment, whether it runs Stalwart or anything else. Users who see the “Try the new Outlook” toggle in classic Outlook should leave it off.

The same applies on phones. Outlook for iOS and Android reach on-premises mailboxes through a Microsoft cloud service as well, which requires a hybrid Exchange Online setup. On mobile devices, the native mail, calendar and contacts apps, which connect directly over ActiveSync, are the better choice.

Late to the party

Section titled “Late to the party”

The obvious question is why Stalwart is adding Exchange protocols now. EWS is being removed from Exchange Online, Microsoft’s own future for Outlook is a cloud client, and classic Outlook is reportedly supported until 2029. Why build support for protocols that will be obsolete in a few years?

Because the companies that run Exchange today cannot wait a few years, and because those protocols are the bridge they need to cross.

An organisation with a few hundred Outlook users cannot replace its mail server and its mail client in the same weekend. It can replace the server, though, if nobody notices. With Stalwart speaking the Exchange protocols, the server moves first: mailboxes are migrated, DNS records change, and on Monday every user opens the same Outlook and the same phone apps they used on Friday. The organisation’s mail is back under its own control from day one, and no one’s work is interrupted to get there.

The client can then change at the pace that suits the users. Over time, as classic Outlook reaches the end of its life and the new Outlook remains tied to Microsoft’s cloud, we expect users to move gradually to third-party email clients built on open standards, and in particular on JMAP. Outlook will stop being an option for anyone who cares about where their email lives, and by then the people who used it will have had the time to choose something else without a deadline over their heads.

Exchange support is not where Stalwart is going. It is how organisations get from where they are to where Stalwart is.

Footprint and modularity

Section titled “Footprint and modularity”

Exchange compatibility is not a small piece of work. It adds about 200,000 lines of code to Stalwart, and we know that some of you would rather not carry that weight if you never plan to see an Outlook client. We built it with that in mind, so that it costs as little as possible for those who do not use it, and so that it can be removed cleanly once the Exchange protocols are obsolete:

  • It is a separate module. All of the Exchange code lives in its own crates, behind a single 
    exchange
    Cargo feature. Build Stalwart without that feature and every line of Exchange code is left out of the binary. The compilation guide shows how. The day the Exchange protocols have no users left, removing them from the codebase is largely a matter of deleting those crates and that feature.
  • It is disabled by default. Even in the official builds, which include the module, the Exchange protocols are off until an administrator enables them. While they are disabled, the server behaves as if they did not exist.
  • It stores nothing until it is used. Outlook and the other Exchange clients keep properties that no standard protocol has a place for: extended properties, reminder state, client settings. Stalwart stores them as metadata attached to the item they belong to, using the JMAP Object Metadata extension, and only writes them when an Exchange client connects and sets them. Accounts that are never opened by an Exchange client carry no Exchange data at all.
  • Its data can be removed. Because the Exchange-specific properties live in that metadata and not in the messages, events and contacts themselves, deleting the metadata removes every trace of them while the items stay intact for IMAP, JMAP, CalDAV and CardDAV clients.

Migration from Exchange and Microsoft 365

Section titled “Migration from Exchange and Microsoft 365”

A drop-in replacement is only useful if the data can follow. Vandelay, our account import and export tool, migrates mailboxes from both sides of Microsoft’s offering:

  • Exchange Server on premises, through the EWS importer, which reads mail, calendars and contacts.
  • Microsoft 365 (Exchange Online), through the Microsoft Graph importer, which reads mail, calendars, contacts and OneDrive files. The EWS importer also works against Exchange Online, for as long as Microsoft keeps its EWS endpoint running.

Vandelay copies each account into Stalwart over JMAP, and the domain moves to Stalwart when its MX records and client settings change. The migration guide for hosted services walks through the whole process, from the first test account to the cutover. With the Exchange protocols enabled on Stalwart, Outlook and the other Exchange clients then connect through Autodiscover and find the migrated mail, calendars and contacts.

Other changes in 1.2.0

Section titled “Other changes in 1.2.0”

Exchange support is the headline, but 1.2.0 brings several other features, most of them developed alongside it because an Exchange replacement needs them:

  • Mail rules. Server-side rules in the familiar “when a message matches these conditions, do this” form, managed in the WebUI, over JMAP or from Outlook. Conditions cover senders and recipients, subject and body, headers, size, attachments, importance, message type, mailing lists, spam score, date ranges and weekly schedules. Actions include moving and copying, marking as read or as junk, forwarding, replying with a template and rejecting. Rules are compiled into Sieve together with the vacation response. Each account also gets junk email lists: blocked senders, safe senders and safe recipients. See Mail rules.
  • Read receipts. Message Disposition Notifications (RFC 8098), including the JMAP methods for sending and parsing them (RFC 9007).
  • Working hours. Per-account working hours, managed in the WebUI, over JMAP or through CalDAV. Time outside working hours appears as unavailable in free/busy lookups, and a new setting lets users of the same organisation see each other’s free/busy time without sharing calendars explicitly. See Availability.
  • Rooms and equipment. Resource accounts with a capacity, a location and delegates who manage the calendar, with invitations booked automatically or approved by hand. See Resources.
  • Native FoundationDB client. A new asynchronous Rust client replaces Apple’s 
    libfdb_c
    library. It needs no client library on the host and is now part of the standard build and Docker image. In our benchmarks it delivers between 4.5 and 6.9 times the throughput of the previous client on point reads, with 5 to 7 times lower median latency and about half the CPU.

The changelog lists every change and fix, along with the upgrade steps: existing servers need to add the new permissions to their default roles.

Stalwart CLI 2.0

Section titled “Stalwart CLI 2.0”

Last week we also released version 2.0.0 of 

stalwart-cli
, and its biggest addition is an interactive console. Run 
stalwart-cli
with no arguments in a terminal and it opens a full-screen interface that covers the same ground as the WebUI: browsing and editing accounts, domains and every other configuration object, watching the dashboard, following live traces and running actions. It is aimed at administrators who live in SSH sessions, or who work on hosts where opening a browser is impractical.

The console is built with Ratatui, the excellent Rust library for terminal user interfaces, and it is schema-driven like the rest of the CLI: its screens, forms and field help come from the schema that the server publishes, so it picks up new objects without being updated. The CLI also learned to sign in with the OAuth device flow and to remember the session, so a single approval in the browser is enough for both the console and every subsequent command.
